Abstract—Crop diseases are a major threat to food security,
but their rapid identification remains difficult in many parts of
the world due to the lack of the necessary infrastructure. In
laboratory experiments, deep models achieve 99.28% accuracy
on PlantVillage, but their performance decreases to 20.85% when
evaluated on field photographs from PlantDoc. To examine this
effect, 298 test leaves were evaluated by exchanging their background scenes with independent image donors. In these donor
trials, changing the background altered the predicted disease
in 21.81% of tests, while synthetic background augmentations
improved accuracy by only 1.24 percentage points (p > 0.05).
We present a streamlined architecture called Gated ConvNeXtV2 for agricultural field diagnosis. The architecture embeds a
differentiable spatial gate inside Stage 2, dampening 81.8% of
non-foliar feature responses. Multi-task prediction heads enforce
botanical consistency across 14 crop families and 38 diseases,
while conformal prediction provides 95% coverage guarantees.
Using a combined benchmark of 6,195 images of diseased and
healthy crop leaves collected under field conditions, we train
a deep convolutional network achieving 85.59% top-1 disease
accuracy (+64.74 pp over baseline EfficientNet-B0), 91.04% crop
family accuracy, and 93.83% taxonomic consistency. Finally,
dynamic INT8 quantization compresses the network to 27.69 MB,
enabling in-browser WebAssembly execution in 73.7 ms on mobile
devices.
Index Terms—Conformal prediction, ConvNeXt-V2, domain
shift, edge AI, out-of-distribution detection, plant disease diagnosis, spatial saliency gating.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Foliar crop infections cause severe yield losses and economic hardship for farming communities across developing
agricultural regions. Over the past decade, deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers (ViTs)
evaluated on curated datasets such as PlantVillage [1], [2]
have reached classification accuracies exceeding 99%. These
laboratory results led to widespread claims that automated leaf
pathology diagnosis is essentially solved.
In real agronomic environments, however, field evaluations
reveal a steep performance gap. When weights trained on clean
laboratory leaves are tested on wild outdoor photographs from
PlantDoc [3], classification accuracy drops from 99.28% to
20.85% [4]. Real field photographs contain irregular visual
elements: patchy soil, strong cast shadows, overlapping foliage, weed growth, ambient glare, and farmers’ hands holding the stem. Under these conditions, standard convolutional
classifiers inadvertently learn spurious correlations between
background clutter and specific disease labels, failing when
presented with unfamiliar field backdrops [5].

Fig. 1. Cross-crop validation matrix of the Unified Gated ConvNeXt-V2
across 14 botanical families, demonstrating sharp diagonal separation and
botanical isolation under natural field noise.

Existing engineering approaches to this problem generally
take two forms:
1) Two-Stage Detection Pipelines: An external object
detector (such as YOLO [6] or Faster R-CNN) crops
individual leaf regions before passing them to a downstream classifier. While effective, chaining two separate
deep networks roughly doubles inference latency, introduces cascading errors when the detector misses a leaf,
and requires thousands of costly, manually annotated
bounding boxes.
2) Synthetic Background Augmentation: Segmented leaf
cutouts are digitally pasted onto random non-agricultural
backdrops. However, synthetic borders introduce highfrequency boundary artifacts that networks exploit without learning invariant lesion features.
Compounding this issue, deep networks output severe overconfidence on out-of-domain imagery [9]. When shown noncrop objects or unfamiliar weeds, standard softmax estimators
regularly output confidence scores above 95% on incorrect categories, making simple thresholding unreliable for agricultural
decision support.
In this paper, we analyze the underlying cause of this
agricultural domain gap and present an integrated single-stage
solution. Specifically:

II. C AUSAL A NALYSIS OF D OMAIN S HIFT
To determine why laboratory-trained classifiers struggle in
the field, we tested whether failure stems from general illumination changes or direct reliance on background contextual
cues.
A. Bidirectional Swap Intervention Experiment
We implemented a controlled donor-swap intervention protocol (scripts/swap_localisation_test.py). For
each test image x, we generated synthetic pairs using donor
leaves from distinct disease classes:
• Leaf Swap (xleaf ): The original background is kept, while
the foreground leaf is replaced by a donor leaf.
• Background Swap (xbg ): The original leaf is retained,
but the background scene is swapped with a donor
background.
• Same-Class Control (xsame ): Splicing donor segments
from the same class to quantify edge artifact disruption
caused by segmentation seams alone.
Table I reports the adoption rate (the frequency with which
the model alters its prediction to match the donor’s category)
evaluated across 298 validation images.
The background adoption rate reached 21.81%, more than
eight times random chance (2.63%). This indicates that standard classifiers memorize background cues as discriminatory
evidence.

C. Hierarchical Botanical Conditioning
Standard models treat plant pathology as a flat 38-class
categorical task, which can produce botanically impossible
outputs like diagnosing apple scab on a tomato leaf. We
address this using a dual-head projection design:
1) Crop Family Head (zcrop ∈ R14 ): Classifies the host
botanical species (e.g., Solanaceae, Rosaceae).
2) Disease Pathology Head (zdisease ∈ R38 ): Predicts
specific lesion manifestations.

V. E XPERIMENTAL R ESULTS
A. Datasets & Evaluation Protocol
Experiments were conducted across three primary corpora:
1) PlantVillage Locked Test (10,709 images): 38 classes
under clean laboratory conditions.
2) PlantDoc Benchmark (236 test images across 27
classes): In-situ field images used for domain gap verification.
3) Extended Field Test Corpus (6,195 images across
14 crop families): Multi-source agricultural field photographs containing real-world noise, partial occlusions,
and diverse growth stages.
Training was executed on a unified pool of 12,000 images
using Cosine Annealing learning rate schedules (1 × 10−4 →
1 × 10−6 ) on 2× NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs.

Field Accuracy Gains: The Unified Gated ConvNeXtV2 achieves 85.59% top-1 disease accuracy on the 6,195image field test set, representing a +64.74 pp increase
over the EfficientNet-B0 baseline (20.85%) and +55.51
pp over ConvNeXt-Tiny (30.08%).

To support deployment in rural areas with limited cellular
connectivity, we quantized the unified architecture to dynamic
INT8 precision.
1. Resolving WebAssembly Kernel Compatibility:
Standard INT8 exports in onnxruntime produce
ConvInteger nodes. In browser-based WebAssembly
engines (onnxruntime-web), execution fails due
to missing SIMD kernels for 8-bit convolutions.
We solved this with a hybrid quantization strategy:
convolutional feature extractors remain in fused FP32
format, while dense linear classification and projection
layers are quantized via MatMulInteger. This produced
unified_gated_plant_cnn_web.onnx (33.8 MB),
ensuring reliable client-side execution in modern web
browsers.
2. Edge Benchmarking: Quantization yields a 74.33%
storage reduction (107.8 MB to 27.69 MB), with an average CPU latency of 73.7 ms in PyTorch and 95.7 ms in
ONNX Runtime. The model is deployed live on Hugging
Face Spaces (https://huggingface.co/spaces/Shubham12864/
plant-leaf-disease-detection-using-cnn), executing zero-server
in-browser inference with real-time canvas-based spatial
saliency overlays.
VI. C ONCLUSION
This paper investigated why deep neural networks trained
on laboratory leaf pathology datasets fail when evaluated in
agricultural field settings. Through donor-swap experiments,
we showed that background clutter accounts for 21.81% of
predictive decisions in standard models, and that naive background augmentation is insufficient to resolve this vulnerability. We proposed the Unified Gated ConvNeXt-V2 architecture, combining Global Response Normalization, stage-2
differentiable spatial gating, and hierarchical botanical conditioning to achieve 85.59% field accuracy across 6,195 diverse agricultural images without external object detectors.
In addition, we integrated Split Conformal Prediction and
Helmholtz Free Energy screening, providing statistical coverage guarantees (≥ 95%) and out-of-distribution rejection.
Finally, real-time edge execution was demonstrated with a
27.69 MB dynamic INT8 model running in web browsers
via WebAssembly. Future work will investigate multi-spectral
